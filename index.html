Old Indian mathematics, turned into rooms you can play in.

Ananta (अनन्त, "without end") is a series of interactive chambers. Each one takes a result from Indian mathematics, writes the math as tested code, and renders it as light and sound. Every claim links to the paper it comes from.

    How to play

    read this once, then every chamber works the same way

    1. Each chamber is one idea. A meter, a series, an equation. The picture is that idea drawn exactly: nothing in it is decoration except the coloured haze behind it, and the trip slider turns that down to zero.
    2. The tutorial walks you in. The first time you enter, a short "how to play" card takes you through the chamber step by step, and each step changes the scene so you see what it means. Press ? any time to bring it back.
    3. The key tells you what you're looking at. Bottom left of every chamber: what each colour and line stands for.
    4. The sound is the math. Every note is picked by a number in the scene, tuned to Raag Bhupali in pure whole-number ratios. Press why this sound to see which number is playing and which note it chose.
    5. You can talk to it. ✦ ask (or g) opens the guide. Type or say several things at once: "show 6 dimensions and turn syllables 2 and 5", "what is a guru syllable", "next chamber".
    6. The notebook has the proof. n opens the history, the math written out, and the papers every claim comes from.
