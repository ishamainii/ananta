02 · माधव · π without end

Mādhava of Saṅgamagrāma (c. 1340–1425) → Nīlakaṇṭha's Tantrasaṅgraha (1501) → Jyeṣṭhadeva's Yuktibhāṣā (c. 1530)

The series

The Kerala school wrote the circumference of a circle as an infinite sum. In modern notation:

$$\frac{\pi}{4} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{7} + \cdots$$

Europe knows it as the Gregory–Leibniz series (1670s), roughly 250 years later [rajagopal1978, roy1990]. On its own it's painfully slow: 1000 terms still get only three digits right. That's what the rose rays in the swing view show.

The end-correction (antya-saṃskāra)

The Kerala insight is that the tail you throw away has a predictable size. Stop after n terms and add a correction to the last one:

$$\frac{\pi}{4} \approx \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1} + (-1)^n F(n)$$

with three successively sharper choices from the Yuktibhāṣā [gyb2008]:

$$F_1 = \frac{1}{4n},\quad F_2 = \frac{n}{4n^2+1},\quad F_3 = \frac{n^2+1}{n(4n^2+5)}$$

My tests (python/tests/test_chambers.py) check that each one beats the last. With 25 terms the plain series is off by 0.04, and F₃ is off by 9×10⁻¹¹.

const CORR = [n => 0, n => 1/(4*n), n => n/(4*n*n + 1),
              n => (n*n + 1)/(n*(4*n*n + 5))];
const estimate = (n, k) => 4*(partial(n) + (n % 2 ? -1 : 1)*CORR[k](n));

Why the chain twists

The chain view draws term k as a link of length 1/(2k+1), turned by an angle θ from the previous link. The end of the chain is

$$f(z) = \sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{z^k}{2k+1},\qquad z = e^{i\theta}.$$

At θ = 180°, z = −1 and the chain folds flat onto Mādhava's alternating series, ending at π/4. Away from 180° the same series blooms into a spiral. Since f(z) = artanh(√z)/√z, it's Mādhava's arctangent series evaluated around the unit circle.

The sound

Each new term plucks a santoor string. Whenever the corrected estimate gains a correct digit, a bowl rings.
