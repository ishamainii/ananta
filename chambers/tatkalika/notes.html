06 · तात्कालिक · the instant and the extreme

Bhāskara II, Siddhānta-śiromaṇi (1150) → the Kerala school

The model

In the Indian epicycle model, a planet's mean position moves uniformly around the deferent (radius R), while the true planet rides a small epicycle (radius r). The true longitude is the mean longitude plus a correction, the equation of centre:

$$E(M) = -\arcsin\!\Big(\frac{r}{R}\sin M\Big),$$

where M is the anomaly. (Sign conventions vary between texts; this is one consistent choice.)

Instantaneous motion

Bhāskara II separates a planet's average daily motion from its rate of motion at an instant (tātkālika gati) [ramasubramanian2010]. In modern terms the true speed is

$$\omega_{\text{true}} = \omega_{\text{mean}}\Big(1 + \frac{dE}{dM}\Big),\qquad \frac{dE}{dM} = -\frac{(r/R)\cos M}{\sqrt{1-(r/R)^2\sin^2 M}}.$$

Ramasubramanian & Srinivas point to the Siddhānta-śiromaṇi passage saying the mean velocity is exact where the equation of centre is extremal (anomaly 90°). There the velocity correction changes sign, passing through zero. That's the idea we now write as f′(x) = 0 at a maximum [ramasubramanian2010]. When the tangent on the right turns flat and green, you're at that instant.

From an instant to optimisation

The green bead on the curve knows only the instantaneous rate dE/dM. It does gradient ascent with a little momentum:

v = 0.86·v + 0.06·dE(bead);   bead += v;

It always ends where the rate vanishes, which is the maximum Bhāskara located. Every modern optimiser, from Newton's method in chamber 05 to the training of a neural network, is built on the same principle.

Why it looks like a rosette

The orbit is drawn from a frame that turns a little slower than the mean planet, and the anomaly runs slightly slower than the mean longitude (the line of apsides drifts). That turns a simple loop into a flower over many revolutions. The physics is unchanged; only the viewpoint turns.
